# Understanding the Plots

This document explains every plot generated by the Hemodialyzer Process Simulator.

---

## 1. Concentration Profiles (Cb(z) and Cd(z))

**What it shows:** How blood and dialysate concentrations change along the fiber length (z = 0 to z = L = 25 cm).

**How to read it:**
- **Red/solid line (Cb)** = blood concentration. Starts high (dirty blood enters at z = 0) and drops as waste transfers out through the membrane.
- **Blue/dashed line (Cd)** = dialysate concentration. Starts at zero (fresh dialysate enters at z = L) and rises as it picks up waste from the blood.
- The **shaded area** between the curves represents waste being transferred.
- The **"K = xxx mL/min"** badge shows the clearance — the key performance metric.

**Three solutes, three stories:**

| Solute | What you see | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Urea** | Cb drops sharply from 1.5 to ~0.42 mg/mL. Cd rises to ~0.64 mg/mL. | Small molecule (60 Da), diffuses easily through 3 nm pores. High clearance (~218 mL/min). |
| **VitB12** | Cb drops from 0.02 to ~0.014 mg/mL. Smaller change. | Mid-size molecule (1,355 Da). Steric hindrance at the pores slows it down. Moderate clearance (~92 mL/min). |
| **Albumin** | Cb stays essentially flat at 40 mg/mL. Cd stays at zero. | Molecule radius (3.58 nm) > pore radius (3 nm). Physically cannot pass through. Clearance = 0. This is by design — we want to keep proteins in the blood. |

**Key talking point:** *"The membrane is selective — it removes toxic waste while retaining essential proteins. The concentration profiles show this selectivity quantitatively."*

## 2. Operating Diagram (Cb vs Cd) — The Equilibrium Curve

**What it shows:** This is the dialysis analog of a **McCabe-Thiele y-x diagram**. It plots blood concentration (Cb) against dialysate concentration (Cd) parametrically along the fiber.

**How to read it:**
- **45-degree dashed line (red):** The **equilibrium line** where Cb = Cd. At this point, concentrations are equal on both sides of the membrane — no driving force — no mass transfer.
- **Solid operating curves:** The actual trajectory of (Cd, Cb) along the fiber for each solute.
- **Circle markers (●):** Blood inlet (z = 0) — high Cb, low Cd.
- **Square markers (■):** Blood outlet (z = L) — low Cb, higher Cd.
- **The gap** between the operating curve and the 45-degree line = the **local driving force** (Cb - Cd) at that position.
- **Shaded region:** Visual representation of the total driving force available.

**The analogy:**

| McCabe-Thiele (Distillation) | Your Operating Diagram (Dialysis) |
|---|---|
| y-axis: vapor composition | y-axis: blood concentration Cb |
| x-axis: liquid composition | x-axis: dialysate concentration Cd |
| Equilibrium curve y* = f(x) from VLE | Equilibrium line Cb = Cd (45 degrees) |
| Operating lines from mass balance | Operating curves from ODE solution |
| Gap = driving force for vaporization | Gap = driving force for mass transfer |

**Key talking point:** *"The operating curve always lies above the equilibrium line because blood is always 'dirtier' than dialysate. Counter-current flow ensures this gap is maintained along the entire length, unlike co-current where it would pinch."*

## 3. Driving Force Profile (Delta-C vs z)

**What it shows:** The local driving force (Cb - Cd) plotted along the fiber length for **Urea**.

**How to read it:**
- **Solid red curve (Delta-C):** The concentration difference between blood and dialysate at each position. This is what pushes solute across the membrane.
- **Dashed lines:** Individual Cb(z) and Cd(z) for reference.
- **dC_max annotation:** Maximum driving force — occurs at z = 0 where dirty blood meets nearly-clean dialysate.
- **dC_min annotation:** Minimum driving force — occurs at z = L where cleaned blood meets fresh dialysate.
- **Dotted horizontal line (LMCD):** The **Log Mean Concentration Difference** — the effective average driving force for the entire device.

**The LMCD equation:**

$$LMCD = \frac{\Delta C_1 - \Delta C_2}{\ln(\Delta C_1 / \Delta C_2)}$$

Where Delta-C1 = driving force at one end, Delta-C2 = driving force at the other end.

**Why LMCD matters:** It's the proper average driving force for counter-current systems (same concept as LMTD in heat exchangers). The total mass transfer rate is:

$$\dot{m} = K_o \times A \times LMCD$$

**Key talking point:** *"The driving force isn't constant — it varies from 0.86 mg/mL at the blood inlet to 0.42 mg/mL at the blood outlet. The LMCD (0.62 mg/mL) is the correct average to use, not the arithmetic mean."*

## 4. Resistance Breakdown Pie Charts

**What it shows:** The fraction of total mass transfer resistance contributed by each of the three layers — for each solute.

**The three resistances:**

```
1/Ko = 1/kb + delta/Dm + 1/kd
       ----   --------   ----
       blood  membrane   dialysate
       film   wall       film
```

**How to read it:**

| Solute | Blood Film | Membrane | Dialysate Film | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Urea** | ~24% | ~69% | ~7% | Membrane is the main bottleneck, but blood-side film is significant. Increasing Qb helps somewhat. |
| **VitB12** | ~17% | ~80% | ~3% | Membrane dominates even more due to steric hindrance (larger molecule has more friction in pores). |
| **Albumin** | ~0% | ~100% | ~0% | Membrane resistance is infinite — molecule is bigger than the pore. No amount of flow increase helps. |

**Key talking point:** *"The resistance breakdown shows that membrane design (porosity, tortuosity, pore size) is the dominant factor. Increasing flow rates has diminishing returns because you can only reduce the film resistances, not the membrane resistance."*

## 5. Feature Importance (ML Model)

**What it shows:** Which operating parameters most strongly influence the predicted clearance, according to the trained Random Forest model.

**How to read it:**
- Bar lengths represent **relative importance** (0 to 1) of each feature.
- Typically: **Qb (blood flow) > Qd (dialysate flow) > Quf (ultrafiltration)**

**Why Qb dominates:** Blood-side film resistance (1/kb) is the second-largest resistance after the membrane. Increasing Qb directly reduces this resistance via higher Sherwood number. Qd has diminishing returns because dialysate-side resistance is already small (~7%).

---

## 6. Actual vs Predicted (ML Validation)

**What it shows:** Scatter plot comparing the rigorous ODE solver output (actual) versus the Random Forest prediction (predicted) for both Urea and VitB12 clearance.

**How to read it:**
- Points on the **red dashed diagonal** = perfect prediction.
- Tighter scatter around the line = better model.
- **R-squared value** in the title tells you the accuracy (R² > 0.99 = excellent).

**Key talking point:** *"The surrogate model achieves R² > 0.99, meaning it can replace the expensive ODE solver for real-time optimization while maintaining >99% accuracy."*

---

## 7. Error Distribution (ML Residuals)

**What it shows:** Histogram of prediction errors (Actual - Predicted) for both solutes.

**How to read it:**
- Distribution should be **centered at zero** (no systematic bias).
- **Narrow spread** = low prediction error.
- A perfect model would show all errors clustered tightly around zero.

## Summary: What Each Plot Answers

| Plot | Question It Answers |
|---|---|
| Concentration Profiles | *"How does concentration change along the fiber?"* |
| Operating Diagram | *"How does our process compare to equilibrium?"* |
| Driving Force Profile | *"Where is mass transfer fastest/slowest?"* |
| Resistance Pie Charts | *"What's the bottleneck limiting performance?"* |
| Feature Importance | *"Which operating parameter matters most?"* |
| Actual vs Predicted | *"Can we trust the ML surrogate?"* |
| Error Distribution | *"Is the ML model biased?"* |
